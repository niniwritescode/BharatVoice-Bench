RESEARCH DESIGN

Behind the benchmark.

The experimental setup, audio preparation, evaluation method, limitations, and future directions.

RESEARCH QUESTION

How reliably does Gnani Prisma v2.5 preserve spoken content across English, Hindi, and Hinglish when exposed to different speakers and controlled background noise?

BharatVoice Bench is an evaluation framework. It does not train or modify the underlying speech recognition model.

01
MODEL

Gnani Prisma v2.5

The project uses Gnani's Prisma v2.5 speech-to-text service to generate transcripts from the prepared audio recordings. The generated text is compared against manually prepared reference transcripts.

02
DATASET

Multilingual recordings

The pilot dataset contains recordings from three speakers: Nandini, Shakun, and Ram. Each speaker contributed English, Hindi, and Hinglish speech, giving nine original clean recordings.

03
AUDIO PREPARATION

Controlled noise mixing

Clean recordings were prepared as mono, 16 kHz WAV audio. Crowd, fan, and traffic ambience were mixed with speech at target SNR levels of 0 dB, 10 dB, and 20 dB.

04
EVALUATION

Speech-to-text comparison

Audio files were submitted to the speech-to-text API. Successful transcriptions were stored and compared with their corresponding reference text.

05
METRIC

Word Error Rate

WER measures word-level substitutions, deletions, and insertions relative to the number of words in the reference transcript.

WER = (S + D + I) / N × 100

S = substitutions · D = deletions · I = insertions · N = reference word count

06
NORMALIZATION

Two evaluation views

Strict and normalized comparisons are maintained to examine the effect of text formatting and normalization choices. Original reference and transcription data are preserved for inspection.

LIMITATIONS & FUTURE WORK

What this experiment does not claim

  • Three speakers are not sufficient to represent the diversity of Indian accents, dialects, and speaking styles.
  • The noise conditions are controlled mixtures and may not fully reproduce real-world acoustic environments.
  • Target SNR is based on the audio-mixing procedure; it should not be interpreted as a measurement of active-speech SNR for every instant of the recording.
  • WER measures word-level transcription differences. It does not directly measure meaning or semantic accuracy.
  • Some API requests were unavailable, so group sizes are not perfectly balanced.

Possible next steps

Expand the speaker pool, include more regional language varieties, add more recording environments, and compare additional speech recognition systems under the same evaluation conditions.
